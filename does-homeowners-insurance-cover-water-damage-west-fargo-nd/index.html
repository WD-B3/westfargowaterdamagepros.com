Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in West Fargo?

The answer depends on what caused the water, not on the amount of damage. Here is how to sort it out before you call your insurer.

Most homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a water heater that fails. They usually do not cover flood, gradual leaks or damage from poor upkeep. The cause of the water matters more than the size of the mess, so the first job after a loss is to work out where the water came from and write it down.

This guide is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Your policy wording controls, and your insurer or agent is the only one who can say what your policy pays.

What a standard policy usually covers

The North Dakota Insurance Department describes homeowners insurance as protection against covered risks such as fire, theft and storm damage. It lists dwelling coverage, other structures, personal property, liability and additional living expenses as the usual pieces. If a covered event makes the home unlivable, additional living expenses can pay for temporary housing.

What a standard policy usually leaves out

The Insurance Department notes that most policies do not cover floods, and that homeowners can buy separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program. Normal aging, maintenance issues and gradual deterioration are also excluded. The department's homeowners page does not address sewer or sump backup, which is the reason to ask your insurer about it by name.

That split matters in a river-valley region. Water that comes in from outside at ground level is flood, while water from a failed pipe inside is a plumbing loss. Telling those apart is the first step, and the flood damage restoration page explains how crews treat each.

What to do before you call the insurer

  1. Stop the water if you can do it safely, and write down the time you found it.
  2. Photograph and record video of every wet room before anything is moved.
  3. Make a list of damaged items with make, model and estimated value, as the commissioner advises.
  4. Keep receipts for emergency repairs and anything you buy to protect the home.
  5. Report the loss promptly and ask your insurer what it needs from you.

Documentation is worth the effort. A restoration crew can add moisture readings and drying logs, which show what was wet, how wet it was and when it was dry. The structural drying and dehumidification page shows how those readings are taken.

Deductibles, limits and living expenses

A higher deductible usually lowers the premium, but you pay more out of pocket when you file. Policies also cap what they pay, and additional living expenses can carry dollar or time limits. Keep every receipt for extra costs, since those expenses cover only the difference between your normal costs and your temporary ones. You still owe your regular mortgage or rent.

If an adjuster's offer looks wrong to you, the department suggests asking for a review or a second opinion. You can also file a complaint with the Insurance Department if a claim was unfairly denied or undervalued.

What West Fargo and nearby homeowners should know

The Red River crested above 39 feet twice in recent memory, in 1997 and again in 2009. Records like that explain why flood coverage gets its own policy and why flat valley land is treated differently from a hillside lot. A West Fargo homeowner with a finished basement may want to ask an agent about sewer and sump backup in the same conversation.

The city has told residents to route sump pumps outdoors and has warned of fines for pumps tied into the sewer. In May 2022 the city reported its sewer system taking in about 11.1 million gallons a day against a spring norm of roughly 2.5 to 3 million. If a backup follows a heavy rain, tell your insurer what happened and what your sump discharge was doing.

Be careful who you hire after a claim

The Insurance Commissioner warns about contractors or public adjusters who show up uninvited and offer quick repairs or settlements. Verify credentials before signing anything. In North Dakota a contractor needs a state license for jobs over $4,000, and you can check it on the Secretary of State site.

Questions to put to your insurer

  • Is the cause of this loss covered under my policy, and under which section?
  • Does my policy include sewer or sump backup coverage?
  • What is my deductible for this type of loss?
  • Does the policy limit additional living expenses by dollar amount or by time?
  • Will the insurer pay the restoration company directly, or do I pay and get reimbursed?
  • What documents does the adjuster need from me and by when?

Write down the answers, the date and the name of the person who gave them. Phone calls fade from memory fast, and a written trail is useful if a disagreement comes up later. If the answer does not match what your policy says, ask for the section in writing.

If your policy has a reporting deadline, treat it seriously. Reporting quickly matters, since the commissioner's office says delay can complicate a claim that would otherwise be simple.
