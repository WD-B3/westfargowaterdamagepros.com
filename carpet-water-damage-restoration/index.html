Carpet water damage restoration is the extraction and drying of wet carpet and pad, followed by cleaning or replacement. How fast the carpet is dried and what kind of water soaked it decide whether it can stay. If your carpet is wet now, lift furniture off it, pull up any rugs and call the line.

What to do while the carpet is wet

  • Stop the source of the water first.
  • Lift or prop furniture, and put foil or plastic under legs.
  • Remove wet rugs, boxes and small items.
  • Open doors and run fans, but do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Photograph the floor and call dispatch.

Carpet, pad and what the water was

Clean water from a supply line or an appliance is the best case. Carpet and pad can often be dried if the crew starts soon. Water that has been sitting, or water from a toilet overflow, a sewer backup or outside flooding, is contaminated, and carpet and pad are normally removed instead of dried.

Pad is cheap compared with carpet and holds water like a sponge, so crews often replace it and save the carpet. See sewage backup cleanup when the water was contaminated.

How carpet drying works

  1. Extract standing water with a truck-mounted or portable extractor.
  2. Lift the carpet corner and check the pad and subfloor.
  3. Remove the pad if needed and sanitize the subfloor.
  4. Float or hang the carpet and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  5. Clean and deodorize, then re-stretch or reinstall with new pad.

The crew confirms the carpet, the tack strip edges and the subfloor are dry before everything is put back. A carpet put back damp can grow mold underneath, which is the reason readings matter. More on drying is on the structural drying page.

Wet carpet in older and newer homes

Many West Fargo homes were built in the past two decades and have wall-to-wall carpet in bedrooms and finished basements. Finished basement carpet sits on concrete, and concrete holds moisture, so dry carpet needs a dry slab under it. If the damp is in a lower level, basement water damage cleanup covers the steps.

Stairs, bedrooms and carpet over concrete

Carpet on stairs and in bedrooms is usually stretched over pad on a wood subfloor, and water travels through the pad into the boards below. Carpet in a finished lower level sits on concrete, which holds water for days and keeps the pad wet from underneath.

Dye transfer is a common surprise. Furniture legs, wet cardboard and rugs can leave stains in damp carpet, so lift them off early. Foil squares or plastic under furniture feet help while you wait for the crew.

After drying, carpet is cleaned and deodorized, then re-stretched so it lies flat. If part of a room must be replaced, ask the crew about matching the nap and color to the carpet that stays.

Keep a small sample of the original carpet before any is cut out. An adjuster or a flooring supplier can use it for matching.

What drives the cost of carpet restoration

  • Square footage of wet carpet and pad.
  • Clean or contaminated water.
  • How long it was wet.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Whether the carpet is replaced or reinstalled.
