Sewage backup cleanup is the removal, disinfection and drying work after wastewater comes up through a drain, toilet or floor drain. Sewage is contaminated and can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so this is both an emergency and a safety issue. If sewage is coming up now, stop running water in the house, keep people and pets out of the area and call the 24/7 line.

Sewage backup: what to do first

  • Stop using toilets, sinks, the dishwasher and the washing machine, since every drain feeds the same line.
  • Keep children and pets out of the affected rooms.
  • Do not walk through the water or try to clean it with household tools.
  • Do not touch wet electrical equipment or outlets. If a gas smell or an electrical hazard is present, call the utility or 911 before anything else.
  • Photograph the backup from a dry spot and call dispatch.

Why this is not a do-it-yourself job

Sewage counts as Category 3 contamination. Soaked carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that touched it are normally removed instead of dried. Crews wear protective gear, and removed material has to be bagged and disposed of properly.

Gurgling drains, a toilet that will not flush, water at the floor drain?

Most people do not say Category 3 water. They notice a symptom first. A toilet that bubbles when you run the shower, a basement floor drain that is filling, or a foul smell near the lowest drain are common early signs of a blockage in the sewer line.

If more than one drain is backing up, the blockage is probably in the main line, not in a single fixture. A plumber clears the line, and the restoration crew handles what the backup touched.

The City of West Fargo bills water and sewer service. If you suspect the problem is in the city main and not your own line, ask the city utilities office. A plumber with a camera can usually tell where the blockage is.

How crews clean up sewage

  1. Put on protective equipment and cut off the affected area.
  2. Extract the contaminated water with pumps and extraction units.
  3. Remove carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that absorbed the water.
  4. Clean and disinfect hard surfaces with antimicrobial products.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, using readings to confirm.
  6. Rebuild with new materials once testing and readings are right.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard puts this kind of water in Category 3. You can ask any company whether its crews follow it. Drying then follows the same rules described on the structural drying page.

What drives the cost of sewage cleanup

  • Category 3 contamination, which adds protective steps and disposal.
  • Area affected and depth of the water.
  • How much porous material is removed.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Rebuild scope for flooring, drywall and trim.
