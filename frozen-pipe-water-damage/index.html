Frozen pipe water damage happens when water inside a pipe turns to ice, expands and splits the pipe or a fitting, and the water pours out as the ice melts.

A flood after a freeze is an emergency, so call the 24/7 line now if water is moving. While you wait, shut the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure, and keep power off to any wet area you cannot reach safely.

Frozen pipe emergency: what to do first

If a faucet runs slowly or stops in freezing weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Do not use an open flame, a torch or a kerosene heater to thaw it. Those start fires, and a pipe that is already cracked will release water the moment it thaws.

  • Turn off the main valve before you try to thaw anything.
  • Open the nearest faucet so melting ice has somewhere to go.
  • Warm the area gently with a hair dryer or by raising the room temperature.
  • Look along the pipe for bulges, frost or drips and photograph them.
  • Call dispatch if water appears, or if the pipe is in a wall you cannot open.

Why West Fargo pipes freeze

The Red River Valley has long, hard winters. Fargo's 1991 to 2020 normals list about 43 days a year at or below 0 degrees Fahrenheit and a January mean near 9 degrees. The pipes that freeze first are the ones in unheated garages, outside walls, crawl spaces and attic runs.

The climate figures describe the risk window, not how often any one street is hit. What they support is a simple habit: know where your main valve is before the first hard cold snap.

The thaw is the problem

Ice in a pipe often holds the water in. A split can stay sealed by the ice itself until the temperature climbs, and then a clean line suddenly opens. That is why many freeze losses are discovered on the first warm morning, with water behind a wall or above a ceiling.

Once water is running, the repair is the same as any pressurized leak. See burst pipe water damage for the supply-line steps, and water extraction if there is standing water.
