Sommerset is a suburban neighborhood in West Fargo, about five miles west of downtown Fargo and a couple of miles from Sanford Medical Center. Listing data says the homes were built mainly in the 1990s, and one listing names a 1993 build.

Elmwood Park and the Hulbert Aquatic Center are next door, and Eastwood Elementary, Cheney Middle and West Fargo High serve the area. Sommerset has no posted boundary, so this page uses those places to describe it.

Two-story floor plans and how water travels

Public listings describe larger layouts here, including two-story plans with four bedrooms, plus three-bedroom duplexes. We do not claim that Sommerset homes leak more than any other area. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The useful point is physical.

In a two-story house, water from an upstairs bathroom, laundry or supply line moves down through the subfloor, into the ceiling below and along wall cavities, so the damage is often far from the source.

That is why the first question on the phone is where the water started and where it has shown up. A brown ring on a downstairs ceiling can come from a fixture directly overhead or from water that traveled along a joist. For the ceiling side of the problem, see ceiling water damage repair.

Kitchens, floors and finishes

Sommerset listings mention brick fronts, bay windows and double garages, but the water risk is mostly inside, where supply lines, drains and appliances run. Kitchens combine all three. When a dishwasher line or sink supply fails, water spreads under cabinets before it shows on the floor. Hardwood and laminate can cup within days when moisture sits beneath them.

Safe steps while you wait

Close the valve

Turn off the fixture valve or the main when it is dry and reachable. In a two-story house the main is usually on the lowest level.

Stay out from under bulging ceilings

Water can pool in a drywall ceiling and drop without warning. Place a bucket beneath a drip and keep clear.

Switch off a wet light

If a ceiling fixture is wet, leave its switch off. Shut off the breaker only if you can reach the panel on dry floor.

Photograph both floors

Capture the source upstairs and each place the water showed up, with the time you noticed it.

Drying more than one floor

A crew usually extracts surface water, removes wet material that cannot be dried, and places air movers and dehumidifiers on each affected level. Readings are taken until the walls, floor and ceiling meet the dry goal. Ask what the readings are and when the next set will be taken, and see structural drying and dehumidification for the full method.

Insurance records

Photos of both floors and a copy of the moisture readings help your insurer understand the loss, but they do not guarantee payment. Call your insurer before you agree to a repair plan.
