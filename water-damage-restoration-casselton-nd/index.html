Casselton is a small city of 2,479 residents in Cass County, about 17 miles west of West Fargo along I-94. It sits at 932 feet of elevation on flat farmland, and winters here are cold, with a record low of 39 below zero. West Fargo Water Damage Pros serves Casselton and ZIP code 58012 around the clock, so a call at 3 a.m. reaches a person and a dispatched crew.

Winter first: what freezing weather does to Casselton plumbing

Casselton averages about 37 inches of snow a year and sees January highs near 17 degrees. A house on a rural lot with a long supply line, an unheated garage or a crawl space gets hit hardest. When a pipe freezes and splits, the water arrives after the thaw, often while the family is asleep or away.

If you hear water running in a wall or see a ceiling stain after a cold snap, shut off the main valve and call. Pages on frozen pipe water damage and burst pipe water damage explain what crews do next.

Water when the lines are old: a town with a long water and sewer history

Casselton installed its water and sewer system in the mid-1920s, and the city later carried out sewer improvements funded by a 2013 bond that runs through 2033. Homes in the older part of town can still have original service lines and drains, so root intrusion and breaks are worth watching. A backed-up floor drain calls for sewage backup cleanup, which is treated differently from clean-water loss.

The City of Casselton Public Works Department provides water, sewer and garbage service and bills for all three on one statement. If water is coming from a city main or a curb stop, tell Public Works. The restoration crew handles what the water did inside your building.

Flat land, spring thaw and where the water goes

Annual precipitation here runs about 24 inches, and the ground is saturated every spring as snow melts over frozen soil. The land is flat, so standing water lingers in yards and window wells before it drains. Check that downspouts are clear and discharge away from the foundation before the thaw.

Casselton sits on the I-94 corridor, where US 52, ND 10 and ND 18 also meet. Those roads connect the town to Fargo, and they are how a crew reaches a rural address. Give dispatch the road name, not just the town.

Until the crew gets there

Shut off the water

Find the main valve, often where the line enters the basement or a utility room, and turn it to closed.

Cut power only when safe

If water is near outlets or a panel, stay back and call the utility. Do not stand in water to flip a breaker.

Move valuables up

Lift papers, electronics and furniture off wet floors, and take photos before you move things.

Protect people and pets

Keep everyone away from water that came from a drain, a backup or the outdoors.

Call the 24/7 line

Say what failed, where the water is and how long it has been there. Crews bring the right gear.
