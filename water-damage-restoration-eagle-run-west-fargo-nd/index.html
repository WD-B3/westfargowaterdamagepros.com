Handling a leak in a shared building

  1. Stop the source if it is in your unit, using the fixture or appliance valve.
  2. Tell the building manager or owner right away, and put it in writing.
  3. Warn neighbors below or beside you so they can protect belongings.
  4. Photograph the source and each wet surface, with the time you noticed it.
  5. Call dispatch and give the building address and the unit number.

Washers, dishwashers and water heaters are common sources in any multi-unit building. Appliance leak water damage covers what to do in the first minutes, and water extraction explains how standing water is removed from a floor.

Ceilings, shared walls and the unit below

Water from one unit tends to appear as a ceiling stain in the next. A ceiling that holds water can fail without warning, so keep clear of any bulge. How crews check a wet ceiling before opening it is covered on the ceiling page. Wet insulation and drywall in a shared wall may need to be opened on both sides.

Drying and records

Equipment is placed where readings show moisture, and readings are repeated until each material reaches its dry goal. The drying steps are laid out on the structural drying page. For insurance, keep your photos and the readings. Whether the building owner's policy or your own applies depends on the cause and the lease.

Shut the valve

Close the supply valve behind the toilet, washer or sink if it is dry and reachable.

Skip wet cords

Unplug nothing that is standing in water. Call the property manager and the utility if outlets are wet.

Protect belongings

Lift electronics and papers onto dry furniture, and leave heavy items for the crew.

Keep a log

Note times of the leak, calls and messages with the manager. A short log helps later.
