Flood damage restoration is the cleanup and recovery work after river, groundwater or surface water enters a building. Flood water is not like a broken pipe. It carries soil, debris and contamination, so it is treated differently from the first step.

If water is entering your West Fargo home now, call the 24/7 line, stay out of the water if it is near electrical equipment, and move what you can to higher ground.

Flood water: what to do first

Safety first. Water that has entered from outside is generally treated as contaminated and should not be touched without protection. If it has reached outlets, cords or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line.

  • Do not walk through standing water if power is on in the area.
  • Photograph the water line and damaged contents from a dry spot.
  • Take only essentials, such as medications and documents, from a flooded space.
  • Call your insurer and ask about flood coverage before cleanup decisions are made.
  • Call dispatch and say how deep the water is and where it came in.

Flood risk in West Fargo: what is known

The Sheyenne River Diversion system became operational in the fall of 1992. Its West Fargo Diversion carries Sheyenne River floodwater around West Fargo and Horace, with about 12.7 miles of protection levees and a stated capacity of 4,600 cubic feet per second for a 100-year flood. Engineers who work on it say it has been used more than first planned.

That protection is for river water. It does not stop groundwater seeping through a basement wall, a sump pump that quits, or a street storm sewer that backs up. For flood zone details at your address, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the city building department what applies to your lot.

Insurance and flood: a point many people miss

The North Dakota Insurance Department states that flood damage is not covered under a standard homeowners policy, and that a sewer or sump pump endorsement is not flood coverage. A flood insurance policy normally takes 30 days to go into effect, with some exceptions. Flood insurance also does not cover finished basement walls, floors or ceilings or belongings stored there, though it can cover items such as the furnace and water heater.

That makes documentation important. Photograph the water line, keep every receipt, and ask your insurer early whether the loss is a flood or a covered water loss, since the answer changes the claim.

What drives the cost of flood cleanup

  • Water category, which for flood water is usually the most involved.
  • Depth of the water and square footage affected.
  • How much porous material has to be removed.
  • Drying days and the equipment on site.
  • Rebuild scope, which can be large when drywall and flooring are replaced.
