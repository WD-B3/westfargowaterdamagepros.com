Musty Smell After Water Damage in a West Fargo Home

A damp, earthy odor is a message. It usually means water is still hiding in a wall, floor or cavity.

A musty smell after water damage usually means moisture is still present somewhere in the building. Wet materials give off odor as they stay damp, and the smell often grows stronger in closed rooms. The fix is finding and drying the moisture, not covering the smell with air fresheners.

Nothing on this page replaces a mold test or a health assessment, and anyone with breathing problems or a medical concern should see a doctor.

Why the smell lingers

Drywall, insulation, carpet pad, wood and concrete all hold water. Surfaces can feel dry while the material behind them is not. Odor sticks around until the moisture is gone, and it may return on humid days. A dehumidifier from the hardware store can lower room humidity, but it cannot dry a wall cavity.

Where to look

PlaceWhat to check
Behind baseboards and cabinetsDamp drywall, swollen trim
Under carpet and padDampness, stains on the pad or subfloor
Ceiling and attic insulationStains, sagging, wet insulation
Basement walls and floor drainsDamp concrete, white mineral deposits, drain odor
Crawl space or utility closetStanding water, wet insulation, condensation
Inside closets on exterior wallsCold, damp walls with poor airflow

Look for visible signs too. Stains, peeling paint, warped boards and dark spots point to long-term moisture. A musty smell without a visible source is often inside a wall or under flooring, which is where moisture meters and thermal cameras help.

What helps right now

  • Stop any active leak first.
  • Open windows when the weather allows to move air, and run fans.
  • Pull furniture away from affected walls.
  • Remove wet items, rugs and boxes that cannot be dried.
  • Run a dehumidifier in the closed room and empty it often.
  • Do not paint or seal over a damp surface.

Cleaning products rarely solve the problem on their own. They remove surface contamination, but they do not dry a wet wall. If the smell persists after a day or two, get the structure measured.

When it is time to call

Call a restoration company if you have had a leak or flood in the last few weeks and the smell remains, if you can see staining, or if a wall feels cold and damp. A crew can map moisture, open a small section if needed and dry the structure. The structural drying page explains how readings guide the work.

If you see widespread dark growth, stop and get a professional assessment before disturbing it. Scrubbing or cutting into growth can spread spores. A specialist can say if testing or remediation is needed.

Why West Fargo basements and winter homes get musty

West Fargo sits on the flat Red River Valley floor, and spring thaw can saturate soil around foundations. Finished lower levels with carpet over concrete are a classic source of musty odors, since concrete holds moisture for a long time and pad soaks it up. See carpet water damage restoration for how crews handle pad and slab moisture.

Winter adds another cause. Warm indoor air meets cold exterior walls and can leave moisture inside the cavity. A closet on an outside wall that smells damp in February may have a condensation problem, not a leak. The fix is airflow, insulation and humidity control, and a moisture reading will tell the two apart.

Sewer smell versus musty smell

A sharp rotten-egg or sewage odor is different from a damp earthy smell. If it comes from a floor drain or toilet, treat it as a drain problem and read about sewage backup cleanup. A dry trap in an unused floor drain is a common cause, and a plumber can check the line.

Preventing the smell from coming back

  1. Keep indoor humidity in a comfortable range and use exhaust fans in baths and kitchens.
  2. Make sure downspouts and grading carry water away from the foundation.
  3. Service the sump pump before spring.
  4. Fix small leaks quickly.
  5. Dry wet materials within a day or two whenever you can.

The same approach works for homes in Fargo and Casselton, where older houses and finished basements share the same moisture patterns.

Cleaning and deodorizing the right way

Deodorizing comes last. Once the structure is dry, a crew can clean surfaces and use deodorizing methods, such as air scrubbers or fogging, to remove remaining odor. Doing it earlier traps the smell in damp materials, and it returns when humidity rises.

Porous materials that stayed wet too long, such as carpet pad or soft drywall, are usually removed. It is more reliable to replace a small amount of damaged material than to keep cleaning something that cannot be dried.

After the work is done, check the area again after a few humid days. If the smell is gone and readings stay at the dry reference, you are finished.

Keep a simple log of the room, the date and how strong the smell is. A pattern, such as worse after rain or after the shower runs, points to the source.
