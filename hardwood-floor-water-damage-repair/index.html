Hardwood floor water damage repair is the drying, and where needed the replacement, of wood flooring that has absorbed water. Wood swells as it takes on moisture, and the damage shows up as cupping, crowning, gaps and stains. If your floor has been wet, stop the source, mop standing water and call the line soon, since early drying is what separates a saved floor from a replaced one.

Cupped, buckled or gapping boards?

  • Cupping: board edges rise higher than the center, a sign moisture came from below or the floor is drying unevenly.
  • Crowning: the center rises, often after a cupped floor was sanded too early.
  • Buckling: boards lift off the subfloor, a serious sign of heavy moisture.
  • Dark stains or black spots: water or mold has reached the wood fibers.
  • Gaps between boards: the wood has dried and shrunk after swelling.

Wood that is dried quickly may flatten again without sanding. Sand and refinish only after the readings say the boards and the subfloor are at the target level, or the floor can cup again.

How specialty hardwood drying works

Standard air movers can dry the surface too quickly and cause cracking. Crews often use mats and panels that pull moisture out through the board face and from under the floor, with dehumidifiers running to keep humidity in a safe range. ANSI/IICRC S500 describes Class 4 drying for dense, deeply wet materials like wood and plaster, and it needs measured goals and longer times.

The crew checks moisture content in the boards and the subfloor each day. Boards are lifted or removed only when they cannot dry flat or when hidden water needs to be reached. See structural drying for the general process.

Winter, humidity and hardwood in West Fargo

Winter heating dries indoor air, and wood floors shrink and gap on their own in cold months. That makes it harder to tell water damage from seasonal movement. A leak in January can be hidden by a dry house, so check the floor near sinks, doors, and exterior walls after any water event.

What to do in the first day

Pick up standing water with towels or a wet vacuum, then lift rugs, mats and furniture so air can reach the boards. Do not cover the floor with plastic, since that traps moisture against the wood.

Do not use a heat gun, a space heater aimed at the floor or a hair dryer to speed things up. Rapid drying splits boards and can lock in cupping. Steady, controlled drying is gentler on the wood.

Mark the edges of the wet area with painter tape and take photos. Repeat the photos the next day. The comparison shows whether the damage is spreading, which helps both the crew and your insurer.

If the floor sits over a basement or crawl space, ask the crew to check the underside too. Water that came through from below can be as damaging as water on top.
