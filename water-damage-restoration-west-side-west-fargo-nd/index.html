West Side is the part of West Fargo near the western edge of the city, a few blocks off Main Avenue and under seven miles from downtown Fargo. Listing data describes a core of homes built as early as the 1970s, surrounded by newer multi-family buildings.

Westside Elementary sits in the middle of the area, and Westside Park and the West Fargo Soccer Complex are within walking distance. Because the name is informal, this page uses those places instead of a boundary line.

A mix of ranches, duplexes and quadplexes

Public listings show a median build year near 1976, ranch-style homes, duplexes and quadplexes, and a median lot of about 10,000 square feet. The mix matters more than the age. In a single-family ranch, water usually stays within one household. In a duplex or quadplex, water from one unit can move into the unit below or beside it through ceilings and shared walls.

We do not claim that older homes leak more or that any plumbing material is present on these streets. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. We can say how the water behaves once it is in the building, and that is what the rest of this page covers.

When the leak reaches a neighbor

A stained ceiling in a lower unit often points to a supply line, a drain or an appliance in the unit above. The page on repairing a water-stained ceiling describes testing, opening and drying before any patch goes up. A sagging ceiling is unsafe to stand under.

Renting or owning in West Side

If you rent, report the leak to the owner or manager in writing as soon as you find it and keep a copy. In a rented unit the landlord signs off on repairs to the building, and renters insurance may cover your belongings. If you own one side of a duplex, the other owner may need to be part of the conversation. Dispatch can explain what a crew can do on arrival.

What to do in the first hour

Name the source

Look for a running appliance, a dripping fixture or a wet ceiling, and shut the nearest valve if it is dry and in reach.

Warn the unit below

Knock on the door of the neighbor under the leak so they can protect belongings and check their ceiling.

Keep power in mind

If a ceiling light fixture is dripping, leave that switch off and do not touch it with wet hands.

Record it

Photograph the stain and the source, and note the time you first saw it.

Drying a shared building

Drying equipment has to reach every wet layer, and in a shared building that can mean a ceiling cavity in one unit and a wall in the next. Readings show where water remains. The structural drying page explains how those numbers guide the equipment.

Documenting a loss

A photo set and readings give your insurer a record, but policy terms and the cause of the water decide coverage. Ask your insurer or the building owner which policy applies before work begins.
