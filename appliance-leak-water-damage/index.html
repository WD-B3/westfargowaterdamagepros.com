Appliance leak water damage comes from the supply hose, drain line, ice maker line or pump of a washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator or similar unit. Water from these sources often runs behind or under the appliance for a long time before anyone sees it.

If an appliance is leaking right now, treat it as an emergency, close its water valve, unplug it only if the floor around the plug is dry, and call the 24/7 line.

Appliance leak: what to do first

  • Close the shut-off valve on the wall behind the appliance. Turn it fully.
  • Unplug the appliance only if you can do so standing on a dry floor.
  • If the valve will not turn, shut the main water valve.
  • Slide the appliance out gently once the water is off and look at the floor under it.
  • Photograph the hose, the connection and the wet area.

Why slow appliance leaks do the most damage

A hose that drips slowly can run for weeks. The water soaks the subfloor under the unit and the base of the wall, and it can drip into the ceiling below. By the time a stain shows up, the material has often been wet long enough to swell and grow mold.

In newer homes, which describes much of the growth West Fargo saw between the 2010 and 2020 censuses, the plumbing inside the walls is often not the first part to fail. Hoses, connectors and drain lines at appliances are, so they are the first things to inspect.

How crews handle an appliance loss

  1. Stop the water and confirm the appliance is safe to move.
  2. Extract standing water and lift the appliance clear.
  3. Check the subfloor, wall base and cabinets with a moisture meter.
  4. Remove wet flooring, underlayment or baseboard that cannot be dried.
  5. Set air movers and dehumidifiers and monitor readings daily.
  6. Repair flooring and trim once the readings are in the drying goal.

Vinyl and tile may look fine on top while the subfloor stays wet underneath. Moisture meters catch what a visual check cannot, which is why readings are repeated each day.

What affects the cost

  • How long the leak ran, since longer means more soaked material.
  • Floor type and subfloor condition.
  • Whether the water reached the ceiling below.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Repairs to flooring, cabinets and trim.

Sudden appliance failures are often covered for the resulting water damage, while gradual leaks and lack of maintenance often are not. Many policies draw that line, so check yours or ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call (701) 495-9075.

Preventing the next one

Inspect hoses and connectors a few times a year, replace any that bulge or crack, and close the supply valves on a washer when you leave home for long stretches. If water has already reached the walls, wall and drywall repair explains the next steps.
