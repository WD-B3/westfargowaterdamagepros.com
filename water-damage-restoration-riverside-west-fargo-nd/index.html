Water Damage Restoration in Riverside, West Fargo, ND

Riverside is a small residential pocket on a bend of the Sheyenne River. Here is how water damage tends to unfold on these streets and what to do first.

Riverside was its own city until it merged with West Fargo in 1989, and plenty of residents still use the name. Public listing data describes it as a few residential streets along a bend in the Sheyenne River, with the river on one side and industrial buildings on the other.

Armour Park links the neighborhood sidewalks to the riverbank. No official boundary is posted for Riverside, so this page describes it by those landmarks and does not draw a line.

The housing, and why it matters when water gets in

Listing data counts roughly 128 homes here, mostly split-levels, ramblers and traditional builds from the late 1970s and 1980s, with some townhouses. The median build year is near 1978. A split-level has a lower level set partly below grade, so water that enters at a foundation wall or after a pump failure usually lands in finished space such as a family room or laundry area.

Whether a particular house has a full basement, a crawl space or a slab is something to confirm at the property, because the neighborhood alone does not tell us. Basement water damage cleanup explains how standing water is removed and how wet walls are opened to dry.

The river, the diversion and flood maps

The Sheyenne River runs along the edge of Riverside, and some homes back directly onto it. The West Fargo Diversion became operational in the fall of 1992 and carries Sheyenne floodwater around the historical footprint of West Fargo and Horace. It has been in service for more than 30 years. That is a fact about the city as a whole and not a promise about any single address.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. To see how one address is mapped, look it up in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and treat the result as a map designation, not a guarantee in either direction. If floodwater does enter a building, flood damage restoration covers what happens next.

Water that never touches the river

Most residential calls start inside the walls. A washing machine hose that splits, a tank that gives out or a supply line under a sink can soak carpet pad and drywall in one afternoon. The steps for those losses are on the water heater leak cleanup page and the appliance leak damage page.

Sewer water is a separate problem. The city has warned that heavy rain can overload its sewer lines and push water back into buildings, and that is why sump pumps must discharge outside by April 15. The city has not tied that warning to Riverside, and neither do we.

If water rising from a floor drain smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated and stay out. Sewage backup cleanup explains why household fans and wet vacuums are not enough for that kind of water.

Until the crew gets there

Find the shutoff

If a pipe or appliance is the source, close its valve or the main, but only if the valve is dry and easy to reach.

Keep clear of wet outlets

Do not step into standing water near cords, outlets or a panel. Call the utility if you suspect live wiring.

Photograph the damage

Take wide and close photos of wet areas and damaged items from a dry spot before anything is moved.

Lift what you can

Move small furniture, books and electronics out of the wet area only if the floor is safe to cross.

How a lower level dries out

Extraction comes first, then air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of drywall, trim and flooring. Walls below grade hold water longer than the surface suggests, so a technician takes moisture readings until each material reaches its dry goal. A wall that feels dry can still read wet. Structural drying and dehumidification explains the readings.

Insurance records for a Riverside loss

Dated photos and moisture readings tell the story of what happened and what was done. Whether any of it is covered turns on the cause of the water and the policy, and flood damage generally needs separate flood coverage. Ask your insurer before assuming either way.
