Sudden Spike in Your West Fargo Water Bill? Check for Leaks

A bill that suddenly jumps is often the first sign of a leak. A ten-minute meter check can tell you whether water is running when it should not.

A sudden jump in your water bill usually means water is going somewhere you are not watching. Common culprits are a running toilet, a failing water softener, a dripping outdoor spigot or a hidden line leak. Before you assume the bill is wrong, test your meter and check the usual suspects.

A simple meter test

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and water-using device in the house.
  2. Find your water meter and look at the leak indicator or the low-flow dial.
  3. If the dial moves with everything off, water is flowing somewhere.
  4. Write the reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again.
  5. If the number changed, you have a leak.

If your meter moves and you cannot find a visible leak, the problem may be a buried or in-slab line. See slab leak water damage for the signs, such as warm spots on the floor or the sound of running water.

The most common leaks

SourceHow to check
Toilet flapper or fill valveAdd food coloring to the tank and wait without flushing
Water softener or filterListen for constant running and check its drain line
Outdoor spigot or irrigation lineLook for wet ground and check hose connections
Water heaterCheck the base, the relief valve and nearby floor
Supply line under a sink or behind an applianceRun a dry paper towel along the connections
Line inside a wall or under a slabWatch the meter and listen near walls and floors

Toilets are the leading suspect. Water running quietly into the bowl can waste a lot over a month and rarely shows on the floor.

Use the city's tools

The City of West Fargo operates a Smart Water Meter System and a Water Tracker, and its Utilities page notes that bills are sent the last week of each month and are due on the 15th. The tracker lets customers set daily and billing-cycle usage alerts, which can catch a leak before the next bill. If your bill looks wrong, contact the Utility Billing office and ask for your meter data.

The city also suggests closing your water valve before a long absence. Base utility charges still apply, but a closed valve stops a burst line from running unseen.

A West Fargo example of why meters matter

A 2022 Valley News Live report described a West Fargo family whose December bill showed about 280,000 gallons used and came to just under $2,500, after bills of $56 in October and $156 in November.

The city's meter data showed a leak lasting about eight days that then stopped on its own. The finance director said most leaks come from a toilet or a water softener, and that a toilet flap can reset itself and make a leak stop and start.

The city was moving to new meters that read hourly instead of monthly, with online alerts for high readings. That hourly data is exactly what helps you find an intermittent leak, so ask Utility Billing for the readings from the days around the spike.

If a leak has already caused damage

A hidden leak that has run for days can soak drywall, flooring and insulation. Look for stains, soft spots and a musty smell. If you find damage, close the main valve, photograph it and call. The water extraction page explains the first steps, and the frozen pipe page covers winter breaks that can cause the same spike.

Insurance rarely covers the water itself, but it may cover resulting damage when the cause is sudden. Ask your insurer, and ask the city about billing options in writing.

Where else this happens

Utility spikes are not just a West Fargo problem. Homeowners in Fargo and Casselton see the same pattern, and the City of Casselton also bills water and sewer through one monthly statement from its Public Works Department.

Stopping the next spike

  • Replace old toilet flappers and fill valves before they fail.
  • Check the softener, humidifier and water filter lines twice a year.
  • Look under sinks and behind the washer and dishwasher each season.
  • Disconnect hoses from outdoor spigots before freezing weather.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is, and make sure everyone in the house can find it.

A fifteen-minute walk through the house each season catches most small leaks long before they become a bill. Take a photo of your meter reading on the first of the month. It gives you a baseline, and it makes a spike easy to spot.

If you find a small leak, fix it quickly. If you find damage, document it before you clean up, since both the city and your insurer may ask for evidence.

What to bring to a billing call

Have your account number, the dates of the high bill and your meter readings handy. If you found and fixed a leak, a plumber's invoice or a receipt for the part helps your case. Ask what the process is for a review, how long it takes and what written response you will receive.
