Basement water damage cleanup is the removal and drying of water in a lower level, whether it came from a sump failure, a foundation seep, a drain backup or a burst pipe. If your basement is taking on water, stay out of standing water near outlets or appliances, shut off power to the area if you can do it safely from a dry spot and call the 24/7 line.

Basement flooding: what to do first

  • Do not walk into standing water if outlets, the panel or appliances are in it.
  • Check that the sump pump is running and the discharge line is clear.
  • Find where the water enters, such as a wall crack, a window well, a floor drain or a pipe.
  • Move belongings to higher ground and photograph the loss.
  • Call dispatch and your insurer.

If the water is up to outlets, call the electric utility to cut power before anyone goes down. Safety comes before cleanup.

Why basements in the Red River Valley get wet

The area is flat, with clay-heavy soil, and spring thaw and heavy rain saturate the ground. Water has few places to go, so it presses on foundation walls and floor slabs and finds cracks, joints and sump pits. West Fargo is also in an area with a mix of drainage and flood-protection projects, which shape how water moves across neighborhoods.

Fargo-Moorhead flood protection has been a long-running local project, but protection at the city scale does not stop water from a failed sump pump or a plumbing leak inside your home.

How crews clean up a flooded basement

  1. Pump out standing water in steps, so walls are not stressed by sudden pressure changes.
  2. Remove wet carpet, pad, insulation and drywall at the bottom of walls.
  3. Clean and disinfect concrete and framing.
  4. Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers, with readings on each wall and the slab.
  5. Rebuild once the structure is dry and the source is fixed.

Concrete holds water for a long time, so drying a basement takes longer than drying an upstairs room. Details of that process are on the structural drying page.

Finished basements and what is behind the walls

Many West Fargo homes have finished lower levels with carpet, drywall, insulation and a bathroom or wet bar. Water in a finished basement soaks the bottom of every wall, and the damage runs further than the standing water suggests.

Take photos of the water line on the walls before cleanup begins. The line shows how high water reached, and it helps the crew decide how far up to remove wallboard. Take photos of stored items too, because an adjuster will ask what was in the room.

Do not run a household vacuum or a space heater on a wet floor. Leave the dehumidifier work to equipment sized for the space, and keep the sump pump powered and clear while water is still coming in.

Once the room is dry, ask the crew to note any cracks, wall seeps or pump problems they saw. A short list of what they found gives your plumber or foundation contractor a head start on the permanent fix.

Insurance and basement water

The North Dakota Insurance Department notes that standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, and that sewer or sump pump backup usually needs a separate endorsement. Ask your insurer which one fits what happened. Photograph the damage before cleanup and keep receipts.

What drives the cost of basement cleanup

  • Depth and area of the water.
  • Clean or contaminated water.
  • Finished or unfinished walls and floors.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Rebuild scope.
