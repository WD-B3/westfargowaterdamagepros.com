Brooks Harbor is a subdivision on the west side of West Fargo, near Interstate 94 and The Lights. Listing data says homes were built mostly around 2016 and describes a community fishing pond, flat lots and insulated two- or three-stall garages. Brooks Harbor Elementary is within walking distance. Next door, Eaglewood sits near the same pond and parks, so dispatch handles the two as one service area.

Newer homes and the rules that come with them

We do not claim that newer homes have more or fewer water problems than older ones, and we do not know which Brooks Harbor homes have basements. What is documented is the rule set around the water system.

West Fargo requires sump pumps to discharge outside by April 15, with a winter variance for sanitary sewer discharge between October 15 and April 15. Properties found out of compliance are charged $100 a month until they comply.

The reason is capacity. The city told residents that heavy rain can overload the sewer system and cause backups into buildings. In May 2022 it reported flow of 11.1 million gallons a day, compared with a normal 2.5 to 3 million.

The city did not name neighborhoods, so we do not link that event to Brooks Harbor. If you are unsure how your pump discharges, call Public Works at (701) 515-5400. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

Flat lots and heavy rain

Flat ground drains slowly, and rain that falls faster than it can soak in collects near foundations, window wells and garage doors. Storm water damage cleanup explains what crews do after rain has reached the inside of a building. Water extraction covers how standing water is pumped out and why the first hours matter.

Sump pump failure during a storm

A pump can stop because of a power outage, a stuck float or a clogged discharge. After the December 2023 ice storm, cleaning crews in neighboring Fargo reported that nearly every pump failed in one neighborhood when power went out. That was Fargo, not Brooks Harbor, but the lesson applies anywhere. A battery backup or generator keeps a pump running when the grid is down.

Do not enter deep water

Stay out of a flooded basement while the water is deep or near outlets and the panel. Call the utility if you suspect live wiring.

Check the discharge

If it is safe outside, make sure the pump hose is not frozen, kinked or blocked.

Treat drain water as dirty

Water from a drain may be contaminated. Keep children and pets away.

Record the water line

Photograph the water level and mark it with tape so the crew can see how far it rose.

Insurance and drying

Coverage turns on how the water entered. Sewer backup and flood losses are often handled differently from a burst pipe, and a policy may need added coverage for them. Keep your photos, note the water line and ask your insurer before work begins.
