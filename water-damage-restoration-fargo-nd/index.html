When water is spreading in Fargo

Fargo losses fall into two groups. Some come from inside, such as a burst supply line, a failed water heater or a washer hose. Others come from outside, when thaw, heavy rain or a high river pushes water toward foundations. Tell dispatch which one you have, since the first calls for water extraction and the second often calls for flood damage restoration.

Older blocks, newer subdivisions and what each does to a water loss

Fargo counted 61,541 housing units in the 2020 census, and they span more than a century.

Older neighborhoods, such as the Horace Mann area, have been kept up or rehabilitated over the years, and homes of that age often have cast iron or clay drain lines, older supply plumbing and basements with stone or block walls. Those details matter after a loss because old drain lines are more likely to back up.

Newer subdivisions on the south side tend to have PEX or copper supply lines, finished lower levels and engineered wood in cabinets and floors. Water in a finished basement spreads into carpet, drywall and insulation fast, so those homes lean toward extraction and structural drying. The Roosevelt area near North Dakota State University has many rentals, and a leak in one unit can reach the units below it.

The Red River, the diversion and what a river-valley city lives with

Fargo records show the Red River reaching 39.5 feet in 1997 and a record 40.84 feet on March 28, 2009. The flat valley floor means water spreads over land instead of draining away quickly. A flood protection project known as the FM Diversion is under construction, and the city has bought out homes in flood-prone areas over the years.

City-scale protection cannot prevent a sump pump failure or a pipe leak inside your own house, and it does nothing to dry a basement that took on water. If river water or overland water enters a home, call for help right away, since contact with flood water can leave contamination in porous materials.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Stop the source

Turn off the main valve or the valve at the fixture. If you are not sure where the shutoff is, the water meter area or the heater inlet is a good place to start.

Make the room safe

Cut power to wet areas only while standing on a dry surface. Water near the breaker panel means staying clear and phoning the electric utility.

Lift what you can

Raise furniture, rugs and boxes off wet floors. Foil or plastic under legs keeps stains from spreading.

Keep people and pets out

Stay out of water that could be contaminated, such as water from a drain or from outdoors.

Fargo utilities, inspections and who handles what

The City of Fargo runs its own water treatment department, with a 30 million gallon per day plant that was finished in the late 1990s. Fargo started with water from the Red River and later added a pipeline from the Sheyenne River as an additional supply. If you suspect a break in the city service line, ask the city water department. The restoration crew deals with the water inside your building.

Larger repairs may need permits. Fargo's Inspections Department checks building, electrical, mechanical and plumbing work against the codes the city adopted, so ask it before you start a major rebuild.

Fargo weather and the seasons

Fargo averages about 24 inches of precipitation a year and around 51 inches of snow a season. Long winters stress pipes in unheated spaces and garages, and spring melt loads sump pits all at once. A burst line in January and a wet basement in April are the two Fargo patterns most worth planning for. See frozen pipe damage and burst pipe damage for the winter side.
