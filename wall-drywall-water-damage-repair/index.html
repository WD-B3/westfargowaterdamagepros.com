Wall and drywall water damage repair is the drying and, where needed, the replacement of wallboard, insulation and trim after water gets into a wall. Drywall soaks up water like a sponge and often looks fine until the paint bubbles or the surface goes soft. If a wall is stained, damp or bulging, stop the source and call the line.

Signs water is inside the wall

  • Paint bubbling, peeling or discolored.
  • Soft or crumbly drywall when pressed.
  • Baseboards lifting or warped.
  • A musty smell near the wall.
  • A cold, damp feel compared with other walls.

Cutting out a flood cut, usually two feet above the water line, is common when water has wicked up the wall. The crew uses a moisture meter and thermal camera to decide how high to go.

How crews restore a wet wall

  1. Stop the source and extract water at the base of the wall.
  2. Map moisture across the wall and the rooms around it.
  3. Remove baseboards and open the wall where needed.
  4. Dry the cavity with air movers and a dehumidifier.
  5. Replace wallboard and insulation, then tape, texture, prime and paint.

A repair is finished only when readings confirm the framing is dry. Painting over a damp wall traps moisture and invites mold. More on the process is on the structural drying page.

Where wall water comes from

Walls get wet from pipes inside the wall, a leaking window, a tub or shower, a roof or upstairs leak running down, or a kitchen or bath fixture. A burst pipe or a frozen pipe is a common cause in cold months, and a wet ceiling often becomes a wet wall, which is covered on the ceiling page.

Exterior walls in cold weather add a twist. Warm indoor air meets a cold surface and can leave moisture inside the cavity, so a wall that stays damp after the leak is fixed deserves a second look.

What to do before the crew arrives

Turn off the water at the source and, if the wall is next to an outlet or switch, shut off the breaker for that circuit. Do not poke a bulging wall to release the water, since a sudden gush can bring down wet wallboard.

Move furniture away from the wall and pull back curtains and rugs. Take pictures of the stain, the bulge and the floor beneath. Mark the edge of any stain with a pencil, so you can see whether it grows overnight.

If you have a small hole already, leave it open for air rather than patching it. A patched wall that is still wet is much harder to dry later, and the crew will want to see inside.

Check the room above and the wall on the other side. Water often travels, and the damp spot you see may be far from where the leak started.

Write down when you first noticed the stain and when the leak was stopped. Those two dates help the crew judge how long the wall has been wet, and they give your insurer a clear timeline.

What drives the cost of wall repair

  • How much drywall and insulation is removed.
  • Water category.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Texture and paint matching.
  • Trim, cabinets or flooring touching the wall.
