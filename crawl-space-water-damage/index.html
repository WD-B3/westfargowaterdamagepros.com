Crawl space water damage is water, dampness or humidity trapped in the low void between the ground and the first floor. The space is easy to ignore, but joists, insulation, ducts and wiring all live there. If you see standing water, wet insulation or a musty smell rising through the floor, call the 24/7 line and keep out of any flooded crawl space that has wiring or a panel nearby.

Signs of crawl space water

  • Standing water, or soil that is soft and wet.
  • Insulation sagging between joists or lying on the ground.
  • Musty air or a damp smell in the rooms above.
  • Floors that feel cold, soft or bouncy.
  • Condensation on ducts and plumbing.

Not every West Fargo home has one

Foundation type depends on the house. Some homes have full basements, some have slabs and some have crawl spaces. If yours has a crawl space, the ways water gets into it are the same ones found in any cold-climate home.

In the Red River Valley the spring thaw raises groundwater and soil moisture, which is the main reason a crawl space may flood or stay damp for weeks. A rain event and a leaking supply line can do the same, so the first step is always to find out where the water is coming from.

How a crawl space is dried

Tight, dark spaces dry slowly, so crews set up equipment carefully. They remove standing water with a small pump, pull out wet insulation and set air movers and a dehumidifier to bring the humidity down. Moisture readings in the joists show when the lumber is back to normal.

  1. Confirm the space is safe to enter and the source is stopped.
  2. Pump out standing water.
  3. Remove soaked insulation and debris.
  4. Dry joists and subfloor with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  5. Check the vapor barrier and ground cover and report what needs repair.
  6. Recheck readings before closing the space.

Restoration is not the same as encapsulation or waterproofing. Those are construction projects that change how the space handles moisture in the long term, and a contractor in that trade handles them. The crew will tell you if the readings point to a drainage or grading problem that restoration cannot fix.

Electrical and air quality safety

Do not enter a crawl space with standing water if wiring, a panel or an outlet is wet. A damp, enclosed space can also be hard to breathe in and hard to leave. Let a trained crew with proper protective equipment go in.

What affects the cost

  • The amount of water and how long it sat.
  • The size of the space and how hard it is to reach.
  • Insulation, ducts and vapor barrier that must be replaced.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Repairs to subfloor or joists if lumber is damaged.
