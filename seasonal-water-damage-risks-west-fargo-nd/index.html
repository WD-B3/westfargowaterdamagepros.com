Seasonal Water Damage Risks in West Fargo, ND

West Fargo has four very different water seasons. Knowing what each one does to a house makes prevention easier.

Water damage in West Fargo follows the calendar. Winter brings frozen and burst pipes, spring brings thaw and sump overload, summer brings thunderstorms and appliance failures, and fall is the time to prepare. Each season has a few predictable problems, which is good news, because predictable problems can be prevented.

Winter: frozen and burst pipes

Fargo averages about 51 inches of snow a season, and Casselton records a January average high of roughly 17 degrees. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces and attics are most at risk. A pipe can freeze for days before it splits, and the real flood begins when it thaws.

  • Keep the heat on and set above a safe minimum when you travel.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during cold snaps.
  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots in fall.
  • Insulate pipes in garages and crawl spaces.
  • Know where your main shutoff is.

If a pipe has split, read burst pipe water damage and, if it is frozen but not yet broken, frozen pipe water damage.

Spring: thaw, saturated ground and sump pumps

Snow melts over frozen ground, so water has nowhere to soak in. Basements and window wells take the first hit. The City of West Fargo has said its sewer system can be overwhelmed by snow melt and rainstorms, which is why sump pump discharge has to go outside.

Summer: storms, humidity and appliances

Climate data show the wettest months at the start of summer, which is also hail and wind season. Roof leaks, window leaks and wind-driven rain are the usual storm losses. Humidity brings other problems, such as condensate overflows from air conditioners and sweating pipes. See storm water damage cleanup and AC leak water damage.

Appliances fail in every season, but summer is when many homes run washers, dishwashers and air conditioners hard. Replace old rubber hoses with braided lines, and check them twice a year.

Fall: prepare while the weather is easy

  1. Shut off and drain outdoor spigots.
  2. Flush the water heater and check its drain pan.
  3. Inspect the roof and flashing.
  4. Seal gaps where pipes enter exterior walls.
  5. Service the furnace so the house stays warm all winter.
  6. Photograph your belongings and review your insurance.

A calendar built for the Red River Valley

Fargo recorded a river crest of 40.84 feet on March 28, 2009, and the area still plans its water year around spring. Even in years with no river threat, thaw and storms bring the same basement and sump problems, so the spring checklist matters every year.

Annual precipitation is near 24 inches, and long, cold winters mean pipes and drain lines are stressed for months. West Fargo's growth, with more than 40,000 residents now, means many newer homes with finished basements. Those lower levels need the most attention in spring.

Seasonal risks for businesses

Shops, offices and warehouses see the same cycle. Roof drains clog in fall, pipes freeze in unheated storage areas, and spring thaw loads parking lot drains. See commercial water damage restoration for how crews handle a business loss.

Where to find help in each season

The line is answered 24/7 in every season, and crews serve West Fargo, Fargo and Casselton. Acting early in a season is the cheapest kind of repair.

Stick the table on the inside of a utility closet door. A simple reminder four times a year costs nothing and prevents a surprising number of losses.

If you are away for long periods, ask a neighbor or relative to look in during cold snaps and after storms. A burst pipe in an empty house can run for days.

What changes for older and newer houses

Older houses in the metro area often have original drain lines, uninsulated exterior walls and stone or block basements. Their risks are root intrusion in drains, frozen lines in poorly insulated walls and seepage through old foundation joints. A camera inspection of the main drain every few years can find problems early.

Newer houses have better insulation and modern supply lines, but they often have finished basements, long runs of PEX tubing and many appliances. The most common problems there are supply line connections, water heaters, sump failures and condensate lines. The fix in both cases is the same habit: look, listen and test each season.

Neither type is safe from a determined winter. A house left empty in January with the heat turned down can freeze in a day or two when the wind picks up.
