Water Damage in a West Fargo Rental: Tenant and Landlord Steps

When water gets into a rental, speed and paperwork matter most. Here is what tenants and landlords each need to do.

If water is coming into your rental, protect people first, then stop the source if you can do it safely, then tell your landlord or property manager in writing. The owner of the building normally approves repairs, and your own belongings are the part you protect yourself. A short written notice with photos and the time you found the water starts every later step.

This page is general information, not legal advice. For a dispute with a landlord, talk to an attorney or a tenant resource.

First hour for a tenant

  1. Move away from wet electrical outlets and appliances.
  2. Close the shutoff valve at the fixture, if you can reach it. If you cannot, tell the landlord or the maintenance line right away.
  3. Photograph and film the water, the source and the damage to your belongings.
  4. Lift or move your belongings out of the wet area.
  5. Send a dated message to the landlord that describes what happened.
  6. Call your renters insurer if you have a policy.

Keep a copy of every message. A text or email timestamp is far more useful than a memory of a phone call.

What the landlord owes

North Dakota law, in section 47-16-13.1 of the Century Code, requires a residential landlord to make repairs and do whatever is needed to put and keep the premises in a fit and habitable condition. That includes maintaining plumbing, heating, electrical and other facilities, and supplying running water and reasonable heat.

The statute allows a reasonable time to fix certain problems, and it does not list tenant remedies in that section, so other parts of the law apply to what you can do if the landlord does not act.

If the unit becomes unlivable, ask your landlord in writing what the plan is for repair and for any temporary housing. Do not assume the answer. Get it in writing.

Renters insurance matters

The landlord's policy covers the building, not your belongings. The North Dakota Insurance Department tells renters to inventory their belongings, and that is also the advice after a water loss. Take photos of every damaged item and list make, model and estimated value. Also check if your policy covers additional living expenses if you must move out for a while.

When the leak is from a neighbor above you

Water from an upstairs unit often comes through the ceiling and runs down walls. See ceiling water damage repair for what crews do with wet drywall and insulation above you. Tell the landlord immediately, and tell them which unit you believe the water came from, but leave the investigation to the building manager.

If your ceiling is sagging or dripping heavily, stay out from under it and keep people and pets away. A wet ceiling can fall.

West Fargo rentals and the Fargo-Moorhead market

West Fargo grew by roughly 70 percent between the 2010 and 2020 censuses, and many buildings in the city are newer apartments and townhomes. Newer buildings usually have modern supply lines, but they often share floors, ceilings and mechanical rooms, so a single failed line can reach several units.

Older houses split into student rentals near North Dakota State University, in Fargo's Roosevelt neighborhood, have their own problems. In those buildings, old drain lines and basement units are the usual trouble spots. Wherever you rent, learn where the water shutoff is, and ask the landlord to show you.

For landlords: act fast and document

A prompt response limits damage and limits disputes. Call a restoration company, notify your insurer and keep tenants informed in writing. Moisture readings and drying logs help show the building was handled properly. The structural drying page explains what those readings show.

  • Confirm the water is shut off and the building is safe.
  • Contact the tenant and ask about damage to their belongings.
  • Start extraction and drying right away.
  • Report the loss to your insurer.
  • Keep a record of every repair, invoice and communication.

If the cause was a failed appliance, appliance leak water damage covers how crews dry the area around it.

Common disputes and how to avoid them

Most rental water disputes come down to timing and proof. Who knew about a slow drip, and when? Did the tenant report it, and in writing? A tenant who reports early and a landlord who responds quickly avoid most arguments. If a small leak has been ignored for weeks, photograph it and send the date.

Another common dispute is cleanup of belongings. A tenant may want the landlord to pay for a ruined couch, while the landlord points to the lease and the tenant's own insurance. A lease may say who is responsible for what, so read it. If the landlord was clearly at fault, ask in writing and keep the request calm and factual.

Finally, ask about the security deposit and the move-back date if you leave during repairs. Get any agreement about rent, storage and return dates written down and signed by both sides.
