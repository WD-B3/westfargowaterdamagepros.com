Selling a House After Water Damage in West Fargo, ND

Past water damage does not have to kill a sale. Clear records and honest disclosure make the difference.

You can sell a house that has had water damage. Buyers worry most about what they cannot see, so the winning approach is a complete repair, written records and honest disclosure. A fixed problem with paperwork is far easier to sell than a rumor of one.

This is general information, not legal or real estate advice. Talk to a real estate agent or an attorney about your own sale.

Fix the cause first

Buyers and inspectors look for the source, not just the stain. A repainted ceiling over an unfixed leak is the worst case, because moisture meters and thermal cameras find what paint hides. Fix the plumbing, roof, grading or appliance that caused the loss, and keep the invoice.

Then make sure the structure is truly dry. Final moisture readings from a drying crew show that walls, floors and framing reached their goal. Readings that prove a structure is dry are explained on the structural drying and dehumidification page.

Disclosure in North Dakota

North Dakota Century Code section 47-10-02.1 covers seller property disclosure for residential property of up to four units when a licensed broker or salesperson is involved.

The seller must disclose in good faith all material facts the seller is aware could adversely and significantly affect an ordinary buyer's use and enjoyment of the property. The disclosure is due before the parties sign the final acceptance of the purchase agreement, unless the agreement says otherwise.

The statute also provides exemptions, such as certain estate sales and foreclosure sales, and it does not require the commission form for sales without a broker, though disclosure in writing is still expected. The text does not name water damage specifically, so ask your agent how past water damage and repairs should be described. When in doubt, ask an attorney.

Expect the buyer's inspection

Inspectors check basements, ceilings, under sinks and around water heaters. A musty smell is a common concern, even after repair. If one remains, read the carpet water damage page or the basement cleanup page to see whether materials were dried and cleaned properly.

Be ready for requests. Buyers may ask for a moisture test or a credit. Prepare answers and paperwork in advance, so you are not negotiating from a blank page.

What is different about selling in the Fargo area

Buyers in the Red River Valley know the area is flat and has a history of high water. The Red River crested above 39 feet in 1997 and again in 2009, and many buyers ask about basement history and sump systems.

Outdoor discharge is the city rule for sump pumps. A buyer may ask where your sump pump drains and whether the system has been serviced. A short note from a plumber about the pump and discharge line is a useful addition to your paperwork.

Selling as is

Some owners sell without repairing. That is allowed, but the disclosure duty still applies and buyers will price in risk. If you choose this path, get a written drying and damage assessment so the discount is based on facts and not on fear.

Owners in Fargo and Casselton ask the same questions, so these steps apply across Cass County.

A timeline that works for sellers

Start with the repair, not the listing. Give the structure time to dry and reach final readings, then finish the rebuild and collect records in one folder. Hand the folder to your agent before photos are taken, so any question a buyer raises can be answered with paper.

If the damage is recent, think about what a buyer will see in the first showing. Fresh paint over a recently repaired wall is fine when you can show the invoice and the readings. Fresh paint with no paperwork looks like a cover-up, even when it is not.

Finally, keep your tone factual. Say what happened, what was fixed and who did the work. Buyers are far more comfortable with a clear account than with silence.

What a buyer's agent may ask

  • When did the water event happen, and what caused it?
  • Who repaired it, and can I see the invoice?
  • Was an insurance claim filed?
  • Has there been any water or odor since the repair?

Answer each question the same way every time, and keep the paperwork nearby. Consistent, documented answers build trust quickly. If you do not know an answer, say so and offer to find out instead of guessing.
