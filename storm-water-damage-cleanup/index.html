Cleanup after a storm means removing and drying water that got through the building envelope during wind, rain or hail. That includes water through a damaged roof, a broken window, failed siding or a wind-driven seam, but not rising ground water, which belongs to flood damage.

If a storm has let water in, call the 24/7 line once it is safe, keep clear of sagging ceilings and downed lines, and photograph the damage.

After the storm: what to do first

  • Make sure the area is safe, with no downed lines, gas smell or structural hazards.
  • Cover openings with a tarp or board if you can do it safely.
  • Photograph roof, siding, windows and every wet room before cleanup.
  • Move belongings away from leaks and place buckets.
  • Call your insurer and dispatch.

Storm damage is not the same as flooding

Storm damage is water that comes through the building from above or from the side, such as wind-driven rain around a window or a roof torn open by hail. Flood damage is rising water entering from outside at ground level. They are treated differently by insurers and by crews, so say which one you have when you call.

A plain roof leak with no storm event goes to ceiling water damage repair, while rising water and groundwater belong to flood damage cleanup.

Storm patterns in the Red River Valley

West Fargo gets about 25 inches of precipitation in a year, and the heaviest months fall in late spring and early summer. That is the period when thunderstorms, heavy rain and hail show up, and it overlaps with spring thaw saturating the ground. No specific storm events are claimed here, since the useful lesson is the season, not a date.

After a storm, check the attic and the top-floor ceilings first. Water that came through the roof often stays in insulation and shows up as a stain days later.

How a storm loss is restored

The first step is to stop more water getting in, usually a temporary tarp or board-up that a roofer or contractor places. The restoration crew then extracts water, maps moisture and sets drying equipment.

Wet insulation and drywall that cannot be dried are removed. The crew documents damage with photos and moisture readings, which helps with the insurance claim. See structural drying for how readings are tracked.

Insurance after a storm

For storm losses the Insurance Department's advice is to report quickly, limit further damage and save repair receipts. A sudden storm loss is often covered, while wear and tear often is not. Ask your insurer about wind, hail and water coverage, and about your deductible. Keep photos and receipts for temporary repairs.

What drives the cost of storm water cleanup

  • The number of entry points and rooms affected.
  • Wet insulation, drywall and flooring.
  • Water category.
  • Drying days and equipment.
  • Roof, window and siding repairs, which a contractor handles.
